The mission

Public construction runs on information that is technically public but practically invisible. The agencies that fund work, the contractors who build it, the brokers and underwriters who stand behind it, and the analysts who measure it all make decisions about the same market, usually with different, partial views of it.

The Concrete Index exists to close that gap. Every figure on this site comes from the public record, normalized and read the same way for everyone. When all sides of the market see the same clear picture of demand, what's funded, what's bidding, what's awarded, what's actually being paid, work gets priced better, capacity gets planned earlier, credit decisions rest on evidence, and capital moves to where the city is actually building.

I underwrite contract surety for a living, which means my job is judging whether contractors can deliver the work they take on. This site is the demand side of that question, built in the open, for anyone who shares it.

What this site actually does

The Concrete Index is a live reading of New York City's public construction market, built directly on the government's own records. Nothing here is estimated, scraped, or manually maintained, every number is computed from official datasets at the moment you load the page.

1 · Pull
Each page queries the City Record, the OMB capital plan, the SCA's construction roster, and the MTA's capital dashboard through their official APIs, the same systems the government publishes from, refreshed daily by the agencies themselves.
2 · Normalize
Raw records are messy: duplicate sub-projects, renewals mixed with new awards, budgets repeated across rows, agencies named five different ways. The site applies one disclosed set of rules so every figure means the same thing everywhere, and no dollar is counted twice.
3 · Read
The cleaned data becomes the measures that matter: the demand funnel from commitment to cash, conversion speed by agency, a composite index back to 2004, and a written briefing that recomposes itself from the live record on every visit.

Who it's for

Contractors see what's bidding now and which agencies are putting work into the market, before it shows up in a backlog conversation.
Brokers & agents see where their clients' next bonding needs are forming, by agency, borough, and category.
Underwriters & credit analysts see the demand side of contractor health: how much funded work is stacked upstream and how fast it converts to revenue.
Analysts & investors get a public-market read on one of the country's largest construction economies, with the methodology in the open.
