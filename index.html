NYC funds construction years before it pays.

The Concrete Index tracks New York City public construction from the budget that funds a project to the payments that settle it, drawn from the public record and updated as each source publishes.

Awarded is a gross figure over the last twelve complete months: it counts renewals and extensions of existing contracts, and it counts single awards of $500M or more. The Concrete Index above and the “Briefing” both hold those out to read fresh demand, so their figures run lower than this one.

Is the City buying more construction, or just paying more?

The headline index counts dollars at face value. Discount each award by the construction-cost level of the month it was let, and the picture changes. The gap between the two is the part of the headline that is price, not work.

Public construction has two sides. The supply side, contractors and their bonds and backlogs, is tracked closely. The demand side, the construction dollars the City budgets, solicits, and awards, sits in the public record but is rarely read, because reading it means pulling thousands of notices and totaling them by hand.

The Concrete Index automates that reading. It takes in the City's construction awards, open solicitations, and capital plan, plus SCA and MTA records, and sets the three stages side by side: capital committed, work awarded, and cash spent. Each stage is measured on its own ledger and its own terms, not traced as a single project's dollar moving through all three. The result is a contractor-attributed view of where New York's public construction demand is forming, which agencies are moving it, and how fast it turns into cash.
